Level 0 · The game in one picture

An alpha is a formula. Every day it gives every stock one number. BRAIN turns those numbers into bets: the highest numbers are bought, the lowest are sold short, and the bets are sized so the book is balanced. Tomorrow's prices decide whether the formula was right. That is the whole game.

Numbers become weights

Six stocks in one sub-industry, one signal value each. Rank it, centre it, scale it. Green is long, red is short, and the two sides cancel so the market itself is not a bet.

Test 0 · Where is the cheapest point?

Three dials, one morning, one hour of work. Which dial do you turn first, and what does it cost?

Days. It needs one passing alpha per day, nothing more. Uniqueness and in-sample need research and luck; days needs an alarm. Every missed day is 0.013 of score gone until the dial fills. We lost 10-02, 10-08, 10-09 and 10-12 to a closed laptop and an expired login. That is 0.05, the gap between rank 238 and roughly rank 170.

Level 1 · Read the five numbers

Every simulation returns a few numbers. Four of them decide whether the alpha can be submitted. Learn them by feel, not by definition.

SharpeAverage daily profit divided by its wobble, scaled to a year. 1.0 is "makes money most quarters". The gate is 1.25. ReturnsAnnual profit as a fraction of the book. Our alphas: 5–12%. TurnoverFraction of the book traded each day. A flag that changes once a quarter: 2–3%. A 3-day reversal: 27%. Gate: between 1% and 70%. FitnessSharpe × √(|returns| / max(turnover, 0.125)). Gate 1.0. The hidden rule: turnover below 12.5% is free, the formula treats it as 12.5% anyway. Self-correlationCorrelation of your new alpha's daily PnL with every alpha you already submitted. Gate 0.7. Since 10-12 the leaderboard also averages this into uniqueness, so 0.69 passes the gate and still hurts you. Sub-universe SharpeSharpe on a smaller, more liquid universe. Gate varies (0.55–0.75). News and analyst fields fail it when their coverage is thin.

The fitness lever, live

max(turnover, 12.5%)
Fitness 1.09 passes

These are the netdebt flag's real numbers at decay 2. At decay 10 the same signal had turnover 1.6% and returns 7.7%: lower turnover bought nothing (it was already under the floor) and the slower weights lost return, so fitness fell to 0.9 and failed. Lesson 14 in one slider: below 12.5% turnover, speed is free, so take the returns.

Test 1 · Fix a failing alpha with arithmetic

A news alpha shows Sharpe 1.42, returns 14%, turnover 36%. Compute fitness. Then: holding Sharpe and returns, what turnover would make it pass?

1.42 × √(0.14 / 0.36) = 1.42 × 0.624 = 0.89, fails. To pass you need returns/turnover ≥ (1/1.42)² = 0.496, so turnover ≤ 28%. We tried decay 20 and 30 and turnover did not move (0.36 → 0.36), because this field's turnover is names entering and leaving the news set, not the signal changing. What finally passed was raising returns: multiplying by an abnormal-volume tilt took returns up and fitness to 1.14. The formula tells you which lever to pull.

Level 2 · The language, nine words

BRAIN's expression language (FASTEXPR) is small. Nine operators and five settings cover every alpha we have submitted.

rank(x)

Cross-sectional rank across all stocks today, 0 to 1. Removes outliers, keeps order.

rank(adv20)

group_rank(x, subindustry)

Rank within the stock's sub-industry. "Cheap versus peers", not "cheap versus Apple".

group_rank(sales/assets, subindustry)

ts_mean(x, n) · ts_delta(x, n)

Average over the last n days; change versus n days ago. Time-series operators look backwards only.

ts_mean(volume,5)/ts_mean(volume,60)

ts_backfill(x, n)

Fill today's missing value with the last known one, up to n days back. Essential for sparse fields: news, analyst events.

ts_backfill(news_mins_10_chg, 10)

ts_std_dev · power · abs

Volatility, exponents, magnitudes. power(rank(adv20), 0.5) is a gentler version of a tilt.

power(rank(adv20),0.5)

a * b (a tilt)

Multiplying a signal by a rank in 0..1 does not change who is long or short, it changes how much. It focuses the bet where the second thing is high.

group_rank(flag, subindustry)*rank(adv20)

Five settings

Universe TOP3000The 3,000 most liquid US stocks. Delay 1: you trade on yesterday's data. Decay nAverage today's weights with the last n days. Smooths turnover. Free when turnover is already below 12.5%. Neutralization SUBINDUSTRYSubtract the sub-industry mean from every weight. Your bet is "within group", never "this sector is going up". Truncation 0.08No single stock gets more than 8% of the book. Pasteurization ONStocks that leave the universe get their weight cleaned up for you.

In one line: rank asset turnover (sales over assets) inside each sub-industry, and tilt the bet toward stocks whose volume this week is unusually high versus their 60-day average.

Level 4 · How we hunt

We did not think of twelve clever formulas. We ran about 400 simulations in 13 days and kept twelve. The method is a loop, and every step is mechanical except the first.

  1. Pick a source, not a formula. A dataset or a field family you have not used: analyst flags, news reaction, option skew, XBRL fundamentals. New source means low correlation by construction.
  2. Screen wide and dumb. One simulation per field with the same wrapper: group_rank(ts_backfill(field,10), subindustry), decay 10. Three slots run at once, one to three minutes each, so a 60-field screen is an hour.
  3. Read |Sharpe|, ignore the sign. A negative Sharpe is a free alpha: put a minus in front. Keep anything above 0.8.
  4. Shape the survivors. Decay (free below 12.5% turnover), a tilt (abnormal volume raises returns; adv20 raises Sharpe but is the most crowded factor on your board), backfill length, INDUSTRY instead of SUBINDUSTRY when the sub-universe check fails.
  5. Measure correlation yourself before the platform does: daily differences of cumulative PnL, Pearson against every active alpha. The platform reads 0.00 to 0.02 higher than this number.
  6. Submit by the rules: first passer of the day under 0.68 goes in immediately (the day is worth more than the alpha). Every later one must be under 0.50 against everything, including today's.
  7. Write the negatives down. A dead field is information: it stops the next person from spending a slot on it.

What is dead (do not re-run these)

Analyst, beyond the four flags

EPS, EBITDA, FCF, FFO, net profit, R&D flags: Sharpe below 1.0 at every decay. Consensus revisions, estimate dispersion (high minus low), up-minus-down revision counts: all under 0.7. The information is the event of a balance-sheet forecast changing, not its direction or spread.

Classic anomalies, plain

12-1 momentum 0.0. Low volatility, accruals, asset growth, net share issuance, buybacks, SBC intensity: 0.2 to 0.5. On a sub-industry-neutral TOP3000 book these textbook signals do not clear 1.25 alone.

Options

IV term slope -0.18, IV change 0.07, variance risk premium pending, 180-day skew -0.93 (flipped 0.93, and it would collide with A1vMp7Al).

Tilts that kill flags

Volatility, implied vol, market cap, price, low leverage: every one of them cut a 1.3–1.4 flag to under 1.0. Flags only like being alone or with a liquidity tilt.

Access

Other regions: not available on this account. The model77 short-interest dataset vanished on 10-13. Check what you can simulate at the start of every session.

What worked, as levers

Decay below the floornetdebt flag: decay 10 fails, decay 2 passes. Turnover was free either way, returns were not. Abnormal-volume tiltts_mean(volume,5)/ts_mean(volume,60). Raised returns on reversal (2.14 Sharpe) and on the news field (fitness 0.82 → 1.14). Square-root tiltpower(rank(adv20),0.5) took the short-interest alpha from 0.74 correlation to 0.67. Tilt strength is a correlation dial. BackfillSparse event fields need ts_backfill or they turn over 140% a day. New datasetThe news field arrived at 0.21 correlation with everything. Test 4 · Choose the order

It is 13:00 IST, nothing submitted today. Four passers, max correlation against your board: A 0.66 (Sharpe 1.6), B 0.48 (Sharpe 1.3), C 0.72 (Sharpe 1.9), D 0.31 (Sharpe 1.26). What do you submit, in what order, and what do you throw away?

D, then B. Keep A on the shelf. C never. C fails the 0.7 gate outright. D and B both sit under 0.50 so they lower uniqueness. A would pass the gate but raise your uniqueness mean; it is only worth submitting on a day with nothing else, to protect the days dial. Sharpe barely matters here: the leaderboard's in-sample term is a percentile of a sum, uniqueness is a permanent average, and the gap between 1.26 and 1.9 is small next to the gap between 0.31 and 0.66.

Level 5 · Submitting on WorldQuant, exactly

Simulations are free and unlimited; they change nothing on the board. Only a submitted alpha counts, and a submission is permanent: it sits in your uniqueness average forever. So simulate greedily, submit carefully.

By hand, in the browser

  1. platform.worldquantbrain.com → Simulate. Paste the expression in the editor.
  2. Settings panel: Region USA, Universe TOP3000, Delay 1, Decay (your number), Neutralization Subindustry, Truncation 0.08, Pasteurization On, Unit handling Verify, NaN handling Off.
  3. Click Simulate. One to three minutes. You can have three running at once.
  4. Results: the summary row (Sharpe, turnover, fitness, returns, drawdown) and the checks list. Every row must say PASS before the Submit button does anything useful.
  5. Click Submit. The platform re-runs the checks, adds self-correlation against your submitted alphas, and either marks the alpha ACTIVE or tells you which check failed. A failed submit costs nothing.

By API (what the scheduler does)

POST /simulations        {type:"REGULAR", settings:{...}, regular:"<expression>"}  → 201 + Location
GET  <Location>           poll until it returns {alpha:"<id>"}
GET  /alphas/{id}          is.sharpe, is.fitness, is.turnover, is.checks[]
GET  /alphas/{id}/recordsets/pnl   cumulative PnL by day → correlation against your others
POST /alphas/{id}/submit   201; then GET the same URL until the body has "checks"; status becomes ACTIVE

All of it runs from a logged-in Chrome tab with fetch(url, {credentials:'include'}). The login cookie lasts about 11 hours and dies with a browser restart, so log in before the window opens.

Time

The dayUS Eastern. A submission at 05:20 EDT is "today"; at 23:50 EDT it is still today. Safe window from India: 12:30 IST to 06:30 IST the next morning. How manyNo daily cap any more. One alpha protects the days dial; every further one must lower uniqueness (correlation under 0.50) or it is a cost. Score refreshOnce a day. Today's submission shows on the board tomorrow around 12:30 IST.

How to evaluate, in order

  1. Sharpe ≥ 1.25 and fitness ≥ 1.0. If fitness fails, do the Level 1 arithmetic before touching anything.
  2. Turnover: is it signal or churn? Raise decay once; if turnover does not move, it is churn, and the lever is returns (a tilt), not smoothing.
  3. Sub-universe Sharpe: a thin field fails here. Try INDUSTRY neutralization or a longer backfill.
  4. Correlation: your own PnL check against every active alpha. Under 0.68 passes the gate; under 0.50 helps the board.
  5. Is it a new source? If yes, submit even at Sharpe 1.26. If it is a variation of something on the board, it needs to be clearly better and clearly less correlated.

Test 5 · The 05:00 IST decision

05:00 IST, zero submissions today, login alive for one more hour. One passer: Sharpe 1.30, fitness 1.05, max correlation 0.66. Submit or wait for something cleaner?

Submit. The day is worth 0.013 forever and the window closes at 06:30. The 0.66 raises your uniqueness mean by about 0.002, which the next clean alpha undoes. The rule is asymmetric: the first alpha of the day is about the calendar, every later one is about correlation.

Level 8 · How to think: the operating system

Everything before this level is technique. This level is the part tutors never teach because they never had to earn it: the seven rules that decide, before you type anything, whether the next hour is worth something.

1 · Name the loser

An alpha is a claim that someone is wrong and stays wrong long enough for you to be paid. Forced sellers, slow readers, over-reactors, index funds, option hedgers. If you cannot say who is on the other side of your trade, you are fitting noise.

Tell: you describe the formula, not the person.

2 · Source beats shape

Payoff order, measured on our board: new dataset (arrives at 0.2 correlation) ≫ new event type ≫ new shaping (tilt, decay, backfill) ≫ parameter tweak. Spend your hours in that proportion.

Tell: your last ten sims differ by one number.

3 · Predict, then measure

Write the number you expect before the simulation returns. Our "this will help" predictions ran optimistic every time. A prediction you did not write down is a story you will revise after the fact.

Tell: you say "as expected" and cannot show where you wrote it.

4 · A negative bounds the construction

Momentum scored 0.0 in a sub-industry-neutral TOP3000 book. That bounds that construction. It says nothing about momentum. Before you call anything dead, name one materially different construction you have not tried.

Tell: the word "ceiling" appears in your notes.

5 · Design for the scorer as it is

The scorer rewards fitness with a turnover floor, a correlation gate, a uniqueness average, and a days dial. It does not reward elegance. It changed under us once; read it daily.

Tell: a number on the board looks "wrong" and you shrug.

6 · Sims per insight

Three slots, one to three minutes each. The unit of progress is not a good alpha, it is a decided question. Queue the simulation whose answer changes what you do next.

Tell: a slot is idle while you think.

7 · The day is asymmetric

The first submission of the day protects the calendar; everything after must be clean. Secure, then explore. Never park a submission behind a question.

Tell: it is 05:00 IST and you are still "almost done".

The thought process, verbatim, for the alpha that passed on 10-13

Read the arrows, not the numbers. Each arrow is a question whose answer was a single simulation. That is the whole skill.

Level 9 · Why signals exist: the taxonomy that generates ideas

Every alpha that has ever worked belongs to one of five families. Knowing the family tells you the operator pattern, the turnover to expect, and who is losing. This is the difference between copying our twelve and writing your own two hundred.

Risk premium · "you are paid to hold what others hate"

Skewness, leverage, illiquidity, volatility. Slow-moving, low turnover (2–10%). Pattern: group_rank(risk_measure, subindustry), sometimes negated.

Ours: vR2xOrRz (negative skew), 786zkWWv (leverage). Failed for us: low volatility (0.03): in a neutralised TOP3000 book the premium is already removed by the group mean.

Behavioural · "people over-react, then under-react"

Short reversal (over-reaction, 1–5 days), post-event drift (under-reaction, 20–60 days). Pattern: -ts_delta(close,n) for reversal; ts_backfill(event, n) for drift. Turnover high for reversal, low for drift.

Ours: XgJ5e7w5 (3-day reversal), VkdvzZa5 and the news VWAP lane (reaction and drift after news). Failed: 12-1 momentum (0.0) in this universe.

Information diffusion · "the slow readers"

Analysts update late; the market absorbs updates over weeks. Pattern: an event field (a flag, a count change) held with backfill or low decay. Turnover 2–4%.

Ours: the four analyst flags. Failed: the direction of the revision, the dispersion, the breadth. The information was that the forecast moved, not where it moved.

Attention and flows · "where the crowd is looking"

Abnormal volume, short interest, option activity. Rarely an alpha alone; the best multiplier there is. Pattern: rank(ts_mean(volume,5)/ts_mean(volume,60)) as a tilt.

Ours: the tilt on seven alphas; short-interest demand (d51v5wpJ). Warning: a shared tilt makes different signals correlate (0.6–0.8).

Accounting quality · "the numbers that lie"

Accruals, deferred taxes, issuance, buybacks, SBC. Quarterly, turnover 1–3%, usually need a tilt to clear the fitness floor.

Ours: deferred tax assets reached 1.36 / 0.98 with tilts, then 0.78 correlation. Failed alone: accruals 0.46, issuance 0.24, buybacks 0.35. The signal exists; the floor eats it.

How to use the taxonomy

Before you simulate a field, say its family out loud. The family tells you: the sign to expect (risk premia positive, reversal negative, events positive), the turnover to expect (and so whether fitness will be a problem), the right wrapper (backfill for events, decay for reversal, tilt for accounting), and who you are betting against. If a field fits no family, it is probably a level (a price, a VWAP, a count) and needs normalising by close, assets or history before it means anything.

Test 9 · Classify and predict the sign

Family and expected sign for each: (a) days since last earnings date, (b) ratio of put volume to call volume, (c) change in number of analysts covering, (d) after-hours VWAP divided by close, (e) 60-day return standard deviation, (f) goodwill divided by assets.

(a) behavioural drift, likely negative (returns fade as the event ages); (b) attention/flows, negative as a signal, better as a tilt; (c) information diffusion, positive if a flag, near zero as a count (we measured 0.2–0.6); (d) behavioural, the sign is an empirical question: a reaction can continue or reverse, and our measurement said the tilted version is positive at 1.8; (e) risk premium, negative or dead in a neutral book (ours: 0.03); (f) accounting quality, negative, low turnover, will need a tilt. Four of six you can call before running. That is the point.

Level 10 · Shaping: the five dials and the turnover test

A raw signal that shows 1.3 Sharpe and fails fitness is halfway to the board. Shaping is the second half, and it is a diagnosis, not a search. Five dials, one test, and you know which dial before you touch it.

DecayAverages weights over n days. Cuts turnover that comes from the signal changing. Does nothing for churn. Free when turnover is already under 12.5%: there, lower decay often raises returns (netdebt: decay 10 fails, decay 2 passes). BackfillKeeps a name's last value for n days after the field goes missing. Cuts turnover that comes from names entering and leaving a sparse set. The news VWAP lane went from fitness 0.55 to 1.15 on backfill alone. NeutralizationSUBINDUSTRY removes the group mean (tight peers, best for fundamentals). INDUSTRY is coarser and rescues sparse fields that fail the sub-universe check (deferred tax, the news VWAP at decay 20). Tilt powerrank(x) is a full tilt, power(rank(x),0.5) a half tilt. Full tilts add return and add correlation with everything else that wears the same tilt. The half tilt took one alpha from 0.74 to 0.67 correlation and another from 1.61 to 1.81 Sharpe. TruncationCaps any single weight (0.08). Lowering it did nothing for us (1.40 → 1.40). Raise it only if CONCENTRATED_WEIGHT fails.

The turnover test

Fails fitness? → compute ret/TO you need: (1/Sharpe)².
Run decay ×2.  TO fell a lot?  → signal churn → keep raising decay until fitness clears (watch Sharpe).
               TO barely moved? → name churn   → backfill 20, then 60. Decay was the wrong dial.
Still short?  → returns: abnormal-volume tilt (full), then half tilt if correlation climbs.
Sub-universe fails? → INDUSTRY neutralization, or longer backfill (coverage).

Level 11 · Your board is a portfolio

From 10-12 the leaderboard scores the set of your alphas, not each one. Uniqueness is the mean, over your alphas, of each alpha's highest correlation with any other. Every submission moves that mean forever. Think like a portfolio manager, not a collector.

The arithmetic

uniqueness = mean_i ( max_{j≠i} corr(PnL_i, PnL_j) )
Adding alpha k with max-corr c:  new mean ≈ (n·old + c) / (n+1),  plus any row whose max rises because k is now its closest neighbour.
Our board, 12 alphas, mean 0.606.   Add c = 0.30 → 0.582.   Add c = 0.59 → 0.605 (neutral).   Add c = 0.69 → 0.612 (worse).
The break-even is the current mean. Anything above it is a permanent cost, anything below a permanent gain.

The shared-factor trap

Three of our alphas carry the same two tilts (abnormal volume × adv20). Their signals are unrelated (earnings yield, asset turnover, leverage) and they still correlate 0.6 to 0.7 because the tilts dominate the weights. A fourth fundamental with those tilts came in at 0.78. Rule: a tilt pair is a factor; use it on one alpha per board.

Compute it yourself (the code we use)

pnl = GET /alphas/{id}/recordsets/pnl   → records [[date, cumulativePnL], …]
daily_i = diff(cumulative_i)             → Pearson(daily_a, daily_b)

What a good board looks like

Level 12 · The machine: operations that never miss a day

We lost four days in thirteen. Not to bad alphas: to a closed laptop, an expired login, a question left open, a browser restart. The days dial is a third of the score and it is pure operations. Build the machine once.

The clock

The day is US Eastern. From India the safe window is 12:30 IST to 06:30 IST next morning. Submit in the first hour of the window, not the last. Login lasts about 11 hours and dies with a Chrome restart: log in at 12:15 IST, every day, before anything else.

The shelf

Never enter a day with an empty shelf. Tonight's research exists to make tomorrow's first submission a one-minute job. A shelf item is: expression, settings, measured numbers, max correlation, alpha id, and a status.

The ledger

Append-only, timestamped, every number. Dead results included. The day you skip writing the negatives is the day you re-run them. Quote the measured value, never "it failed".

Pre-registration

Before a wave: what you expect, why, and what result would kill the idea. Three lines. Our optimism bias was consistent; the written prediction is the only cure.

Three failures we hit, and the fix

Hidden tab freezes timers: run the scheduler from a page with no background traffic and advance it from your own calls. A timed-out submit executed anyway: read status before re-posting. Browser restart drops the cookie: state lives in localStorage on the API origin, so the queue survives; the login does not.

The five-line checkpoint

Objective · Done · Next · Parked · Blocked. Written at the end of every run, in that order, nothing else. If a reader cannot act from those five lines, the run is not finished.

Level 14 · Constructions we have not built yet

Everything on the board uses nine operators. The language has more, and the datasets have 3,000 fields we never touched. This is the frontier: each card is a construction with a story, an expression to try, and the number that would make it real.

Vector fields: the 800 we skipped

news12 and analyst4 carry per-item vectors (one value per news story, per broker estimate). They need vector operators before anything else: vec_avg, vec_sum, vec_count, vec_max. The count alone is attention; the dispersion across items is disagreement.

group_rank(ts_backfill(vec_count(news_vector_field),10), subindustry)
group_rank(vec_max(field)-vec_avg(field), subindustry)

Make it real: any vector construction above 1.0 Sharpe with turnover under 0.3.

Event conditioning

Trade a slow signal only when a fast one confirms. if_else(cond, a, b) and trade_when(enter, alpha, exit) let you hold a position from an event until a condition.

trade_when(ts_backfill(anl4_ptp_flag,1)>0, group_rank(news_mins_10_chg, subindustry), ts_delta(close,20)<-0.1)

Make it real: Sharpe above the unconditioned version and correlation under 0.5 with both parents.

Interactions of two sources

Multiply two uncorrelated live signals. We tried one (news reaction × ptp flag): 1.43 / 0.92. The product focuses on stocks where both say yes. Correlation with each parent is usually 0.5–0.6: only worth it if it clears fitness and both parents are not on the board.

Make it real: fitness over 1.0 and under 0.5 against everything.

Multi-horizon blends

Sum the rank of a signal at two horizons: group_rank(x,sub) + group_rank(ts_mean(x,60),sub). Raises Sharpe, raises correlation with the single-horizon parent. Use when the parent is not submitted.

Cross-sectional alternatives to rank

group_zscore keeps magnitude (an extreme value gets an extreme weight); ts_rank(x, n) ranks a stock against its own history instead of its peers (a 52-week-high style signal; ts_max was not available to us, ts_rank is). Try both on any signal that passes with group_rank: a different weighting is a cheap decorrelation.
